# 5-Minute Interview Prep

**Read this in the parking lot.** Mercedes-Benz sales floor. You've done the work —
this is just loading it into the front of your head. Numbers first, story second,
close last. Ask for the job before you leave the room.

---

## The 30-second answer to "tell me about yourself"

> "I come from brand and communications — I've spent years figuring out how to make
> a message land so it can't be misread, and how to follow up until it converts.
> I'm bringing that same discipline to the floor. I don't wing it. I work a plan:
> a unit goal, a source mix, a follow-up cadence that never lets a prospect go cold,
> and I use every tool available to send eight thoughtful touches in the time most
> people send one. I'm here because Mercedes is a product I can sell with conviction,
> and because this is a numbers game I already know how to win."

Say it in your own words. The point is: **systematic, disciplined, hungry — not a floor-up gambler.**

---

## The six questions, and your spine for each

**1. "Why cars? Why sales?"**
Sales rewards exactly what I'm good at: preparation, follow-up, and reading a person.
I'd rather be paid on what I produce than on showing up. Cars because the product is
real, the customer is emotional in a good way, and one relationship becomes a
referral tree.

**2. "Why Mercedes-Benz / why this store?"**
The product sells itself on quality — my job is to remove friction, not oversell.
And Mercedes has something most brands don't: **the Sprinter commercial line.**
That's a whole B2B pipeline sitting next to the retail floor that most salespeople
never touch. I want a store that lets me work both.

**3. "How would you hit your number?"**
By not treating every month as a coin flip. I set a unit goal and back into it with a
source mix — equity/lease-end, floor ups, internet/CRM, repeat & referral, service
drive — so I'm never dependent on walk-in luck. Then a daily activity floor:
**outbound touches, live conversations, appointments set.** Activity I control;
the units follow.

**4. "Walk me through how you follow up."**
Most deals die in follow-up, not on the floor. I run a **fixed cadence — day 1, 2, 4,
7, 14, 21, 30** — and every touch carries a payload: a specific alternative structure,
a different trim, a reason to act this month. Never "just checking in." I track it so a
prospect I haven't reached goes red instead of quietly aging out.

**5. "Tell me about a tough close / an objection you handled."**
Use STAR, keep it 45 seconds. If you need a real hook: talk about **payment
objections** — customer loved the car, said the payment was ~$80/month too high.
Instead of caving on price, I re-structured: showed the same money on a different term,
a different trim, or an alternate unit — and I could tell them fast, on the phone,
what discount a target payment actually required *before* walking to the desk. Value,
not desperation.

**6. "What's your weakness / where do you need to grow?"**
Be honest and forward-looking: still learning the product line and the finance
programs cold, and I close that gap by drilling residuals, money factors, and the
current programs so I'm never guessing in front of a customer. **AI drafts, I verify —
I never quote a number I haven't checked against the desk.**

## Numbers to own cold (say them without notes)

- **Goal:** 15 units/month, built from a real source mix — not hope.
- **Daily floor:** ~40 outbound touches, ~10 live conversations, 2 appointments set.
- **Follow-up cadence:** 1 · 2 · 4 · 7 · 14 · 21 · 30 days, keyed to touches completed.
- **The Sprinter goldmine:** a franchisee of a qualified brand gets **Medium-Fleet
  cash on a single van** — thousands per deal — and most reps never ask "who's your
  parent company?" That one question is the edge.
- **Quoting:** I can ballpark a lease or finance payment on the phone, and solve
  *backward* from a target payment to the discount it needs — so I know instantly
  whether an advertised payment is reachable on a specific unit. (Estimate only; the
  desk is the source of truth.)

If they hand you a real scenario, reason out loud: MSRP → term → residual + money
factor → payment. You don't need the exact figure; you need to sound like someone who
knows *how* the number is built.

---

## Your one differentiator — plant it on purpose

You are not another floor-up gambler. You're the rep who:

1. **Prospects commercial.** The Sprinter/CBSP franchise angle is a B2B pipeline
   running next to retail. Fleet cash on a single van closes deals nobody else is even
   calling on.
2. **Never lets a lead die.** A written cadence and a system, not sticky notes.
3. **Uses AI as leverage, ethically.** Faster, more personal follow-up — drafts I
   verify, PII stays in the CRM, no fake urgency, no dialing scripts. Speed and
   preparation, not deception.

Land at least one of these in the interview. It's the reason to hire *you*.

---

## Four questions to ask them (pick two)

- "What separates your top producer from the middle of the board here?"
- "How does the store handle commercial / Sprinter leads — is that mine to prospect,
  or is it funneled?"
- "What does great onboarding look like in the first 90 days?"
- "How are internet leads distributed, and what's the expected response time?"

Asking about *how leads flow* signals you already think like a producer.

---

## The close (do not skip this)

Before you stand up:

> "This is the job I want. I run a plan, I follow up harder than anyone, and I'll
> prospect business nobody else is calling. What are the next steps — and is there
> anything giving you hesitation I can address right now?"

Then be quiet. Let them answer.

---

## Don'ts

- Don't bad-mouth other brands or other stores.
- Don't promise a payment or a rate — "I'd confirm that with the desk before I ever
  told a customer."
- Don't oversell yourself past the truth. Systematic + hungry beats a highlight reel.
- Don't leave without asking for the job.

*You built the system. Now go run the play.*
