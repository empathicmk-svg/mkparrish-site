5:00 Pre-interview prep · read before you walk in

Mercedes-Benz · Sales Floor

Load it in. Then go run the play.

Numbers first. Story second. Ask for the job before you leave the room.

The opener

“Tell me about yourself” — 30 seconds

Say something like “I come from brand and communications — years spent making a message land so it can’t be misread, and following up until it converts. I bring that same discipline to the floor. I don’t wing it: a unit goal, a source mix, a follow-up cadence that never lets a prospect go cold, and every tool available to send eight thoughtful touches in the time most people send one. I’m here because Mercedes is a product I can sell with conviction — and this is a numbers game I already know how to win.”

The point to land: systematic, disciplined, hungry — not a floor-up gambler.

The six questions

Your spine for each

  1. Why cars? Why sales?

    Sales rewards what I’m good at: preparation, follow-up, reading a person. I’d rather be paid on what I produce than on showing up. Cars because the product is real, the customer is emotional in a good way, and one relationship becomes a referral tree.

  2. Why Mercedes-Benz? Why this store?

    The product sells itself on quality — my job is to remove friction, not oversell. And Mercedes has the Sprinter commercial line: a whole B2B pipeline next to the retail floor that most salespeople never touch. I want a store that lets me work both.

  3. How would you hit your number?

    By not treating every month as a coin flip. A unit goal, backed into with a source mix — equity/lease-end, floor ups, internet/CRM, repeat & referral, service drive — so I’m never dependent on walk-in luck. Then a daily activity floor. Activity I control; the units follow.

  4. Walk me through how you follow up.

    Most deals die in follow-up, not on the floor. A fixed cadence — day 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 21, 30 — and every touch carries a payload: a specific alternative structure, a different trim, a reason to act this month. Never “just checking in.” If I haven’t reached someone, they go red instead of aging out.

  5. A tough close or objection you handled.

    STAR, 45 seconds. Hook: a payment objection — loved the car, said the payment was ~$80/mo too high. Instead of caving on price, I re-structured — same money on a different term, trim, or unit — and could tell them fast, on the phone, what a target payment actually required before walking to the desk. Value, not desperation.

  6. Your weakness / where you need to grow.

    Honest and forward-looking: still learning the product line and finance programs cold — and I close that gap by drilling residuals, money factors, and current programs. AI drafts, I verify. I never quote a number I haven’t checked against the desk.

Own these cold · no notes

Plant it on purpose

Your one differentiator

You’re not another floor-up gambler. Land at least one of these — it’s the reason to hire you.

  • Commercial

    You prospect business. The Sprinter / franchise angle is a B2B pipeline running next to retail. Fleet cash closes deals nobody else is even calling on.

  • System

    No lead dies. A written cadence and a real follow-up engine — not sticky notes.

  • AI edge

    Leverage, ethically. Faster, more personal follow-up — drafts I verify, PII stays in the CRM, no fake urgency. Speed and preparation, not deception.

Turn it around · pick two

Ask them

  • What separates your top producer from the middle of the board here?
  • How does the store handle commercial / Sprinter leads — mine to prospect, or funneled?
  • What does great onboarding look like in the first 90 days?
  • How are internet leads distributed, and what’s the expected response time?

Asking how leads flow signals you already think like a producer.

Do not skip this

The close

Before you stand up

“This is the job I want. I run a plan, I follow up harder than anyone, and I’ll prospect business nobody else is calling. What are the next steps — and is there anything giving you hesitation I can address right now?”

Then be quiet. Let them answer.

Keep it clean

Don’ts

  • Don’t bad-mouth other brands or other stores.
  • Don’t promise a payment or a rate — “I’d confirm that with the desk first.”
  • Don’t oversell past the truth. Systematic + hungry beats a highlight reel.
  • Don’t leave without asking for the job.
