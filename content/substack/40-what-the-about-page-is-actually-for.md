The About page is one of the most-visited pages on almost every website, and almost everyone writes it last, fastest, and worst. It gets treated as an obligation — a place to list the founding year, the mission statement, the team photos, the "we're passionate about X" boilerplate. And then companies are puzzled that it doesn't seem to *do* anything. But the About page is doing more work than they think; they've just misunderstood the job. The About page is not where you talk about yourself. It's where a wary buyer goes to decide whether they trust you — and it either earns that trust or quietly loses it.

Here's the thing people miss about who reads the About page and why. Nobody lands on your About page idly. They go there at a *specific moment in the decision*: they're interested, but not yet convinced, and they want to know who's behind this before they commit. They're looking for reasons to trust you — competence, values, a sense that there are real, credible humans here who understand their problem. Or they're looking,半-consciously, for reasons to bail. The About page catches the buyer mid-doubt, which makes it one of the highest-stakes pages you own — and "founded in 2019, passionate about innovation" answers none of the questions they actually arrived with.

> The About page isn't about you. It's about whether the reader should trust you — and they decide that not from your mission statement, but from whether you clearly understand *their* problem and have credible reason to be the one solving it.

The reframe that fixes most About pages is simple: stop answering "who are we?" and start answering "why should you trust us with this?" Those produce completely different pages. The first produces a corporate autobiography nobody asked for. The second produces a page that connects your story to the reader's need — that uses your background, your origin, your point of view as *evidence* that you're the right choice. Everything on the page should be earning trust toward a decision, not just informing.

## The Jobs of a Real About Page

A strong About page does a few specific things, and you can audit yours against them:

1. **Prove you understand the reader's problem.** Before anything about you, signal that you *get it* — that you understand the situation, the frustration, the stakes the reader is living with. Trust starts with feeling understood. An About page that opens by demonstrating you know the reader's world is already ahead of every "our journey began in 2019."
2. **Earn credibility with specifics, not adjectives.** This is where your real proof goes — the experience, the track record, the specific things that make you qualified. Not "seasoned experts" but the actual receipts. The About page is allowed to make the case for your competence directly; just make it with evidence a skeptic would accept.
3. **Show the point of view.** People trust people who clearly believe something. Your About page should make the reader feel they know how you think about the work — what you stand for, what you refuse to do. That perspective is the part no competitor can copy, and it's often what tips a wary reader into trust. (See "The Story You Stopped Telling" — the real origin belongs here.)
4. **Make it human enough to believe.** Real names, real faces, a real voice. Buyers trust humans, not "the team." A page that feels like actual people stand behind it — with a story, a perspective, and skin in the game — converts the doubt that the About page exists to catch.

Notice the tension running through all of this: the About page is the one place you're *allowed* to talk about yourself, and the trap is doing exactly that without connecting any of it to the reader. The skill is using your story as evidence for *their* decision. Every fact about you should be implicitly answering the reader's real question — "can I trust these people with my problem?" — not just adding to your autobiography.

There's a reason this page rewards effort out of all proportion to its length. By the time someone reaches your About page, they're warm — they've already shown interest. The About page is often the last thing between interest and action, the place where trust either closes the gap or fails to. A homepage gets skimmed by everyone; the About page gets *read* by the people closest to buying. Writing it last and fastest means writing your highest-intent readers a worst-effort page.

So go read your About page as the wary-but-interested buyer who actually visits it — someone deciding whether to trust you, looking for a reason either way. Does it understand them, prove you, and feel human? Or is it a corporate autobiography written out of obligation? It's not about you. It never was. It's about whether they can trust you — so write it like the trust is on the line, because it is.
