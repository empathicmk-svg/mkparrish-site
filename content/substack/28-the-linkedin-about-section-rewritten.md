Your LinkedIn About section is read more than your website and written with a fraction of the care. Every prospect, recruiter, partner, and potential client opens it before they decide whether you're worth a conversation. It is, for most professionals, the single highest-traffic piece of personal copy they own — and it almost always opens with "Accomplished professional with a passion for..." This paid piece does two things: tears down a real (anonymized) About section line by line, then hands you a fill-in-the-blanks template so you can rebuild yours in an hour.

Here's the patient — a composite of the dozens I've rewritten, every cliché intact:

> *Accomplished marketing leader with over 12 years of experience driving growth for innovative companies. Passionate about leveraging data-driven strategies to deliver results. Proven track record of building high-performing teams and exceeding targets. Always learning, always growing. Let's connect!*

Five sentences, zero information. You finish it knowing nothing specific about this person — not what they're great at, not who they help, not what they believe. It's a paragraph-shaped fog. And it's *typical,* which is the opportunity: in a feed full of identical fog, a specific About section is almost unfairly conspicuous.

> Your About section's job is not to summarize your career. It's to make the right reader think "this is exactly the person I need" within the first two lines — before LinkedIn truncates the rest behind "see more." If the hook isn't in the first two lines, it doesn't exist.

## The Teardown

**"Accomplished marketing leader with over 12 years of experience..."** Opens with a credential and a category — the two least distinguishing things you can lead with, and the most common. Worse, it's buried in the only two lines LinkedIn shows before "see more," which means your most valuable real estate is spent on a phrase identical to ten thousand other profiles. The hook is missing at the exact spot the hook has to be.

**"Passionate about leveraging data-driven strategies to deliver results."** Three category clichés in one sentence: "passionate," "leveraging," "data-driven," "deliver results." Each is unverifiable, transferable, and dead. "Passionate about" tells the reader nothing — everyone claims passion. The sentence could belong to anyone in marketing, which means it does the work of distinguishing you from no one.

**"Proven track record of building high-performing teams and exceeding targets."** "Proven track record" is the tell of an unproven claim — if it were really proven, you'd show the proof instead of asserting the abstraction. Replace it with one real number or named outcome and it does ten times the work.

**"Always learning, always growing. Let's connect!"** Filler and a weak CTA. "Always learning" is a participation trophy. "Let's connect!" asks for nothing specific and gives the reader no reason. The close should tell the right person exactly what to do and why.

## The Rebuild

A strong About section has a five-part structure. Here it is, with the patient rebuilt:

1. **The hook (first two lines — the only ones that show).** Lead with the specific thing you do and who you do it for, framed as a claim or an intriguing tension. *"I help B2B founders whose product is better than their marketing makes it sound. Usually that means the homepage is the problem — and the homepage is fixable."*
2. **The proof (one specific receipt).** One concrete, checkable outcome. *"Last year I rewrote positioning for 14 companies; the through-line was the same — sharper words, not more of them. One doubled inbound demos without touching ad spend."*
3. **The point of view (what you believe).** The thing that makes you *you,* not just competent. *"I think most 'marketing problems' are actually clarity problems. You don't need more content. You need to decide what you're for and say it like you mean it."*
4. **The proof of person (a real, human detail).** Something true that a generic profile wouldn't include — the throughline of why you do this. *"I learned this the hard way, rewriting my own story before I ever rewrote anyone else's."*
5. **The close (a specific next step).** Tell the right reader exactly what to do. *"If your homepage doesn't sound as good as your product actually is, my DMs are open — or the link below."*

Stitched together, that's a paragraph that informs, persuades, sounds like a person, and tells the reader what to do — everything the original fog did not.

## The fill-in-the-blanks template

Copy this and replace the brackets with *specific, true* answers:

> *I help [specific audience] who [specific situation/problem]. Usually that means [the specific thing you actually do].*
> *[One concrete proof point — a number, a named outcome, a specific transformation.]*
> *I believe [your contrarian-but-defensible point of view about your work — the thing not everyone in your field would say].*
> *[One true, human detail about why you do this — the throughline.]*
> *If [the reader's specific situation], [specific, low-friction next step].*

Two rules while you fill it in. **First: pass the competitor test on every line** — if a peer could write the same sentence, it's not specific enough yet. **Second: the first two lines have to hook by themselves,** because that's all most people read before scrolling. Write those two like they're the whole thing, because functionally they are.

Run it this week. The About section is the cheapest, highest-traffic rewrite available to you — fifteen minutes of specific replaces years of fog, in the one place nearly everyone who's deciding about you actually looks.
