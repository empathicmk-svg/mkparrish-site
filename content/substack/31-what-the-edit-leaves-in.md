---
title: What the Edit Leaves In
subtitle: On subtraction as an act of love, the courage of the cut, and the few true lines that survive when everything safe is taken away.
category: Poetry

The edit is not what you add. It's what stays
when you've cut every word that was hiding the blaze.
It's the nerve to take out what was pretty but small
so the one living sentence can stand up at all.

I crossed out the safe lines. They cost me the most —
not the bad ones, the *fine* ones, the comfortable host
of the words that said nothing but said it so well
no one would have flinched, and no one could tell.

But I would have known. And the knowing's the thing.
So I cut what was hollow to keep what could sting,
kept the line that exposed me, the one that was true,
the sentence I'd marked *too much* — and left it in view.

This is the work, and it's lonelier than it looks:
the taking-away, the white space, the rebuke
of your own better instincts to soften, to pad,
to keep the room cozy and never get sad.

The edit says *less.* The edit says *no.*
The edit says *that part you love? Let it go,
if it's serving your comfort and not serving the page.*
And what's left when you're done is the thing with the rage,

the tenderness, the specific, the strange —
the few honest lines that could not be arranged
out of anything other than your one only life.
That's what the edit leaves in. That's the knife

turned, at the end, into something like grace:
not the cluttered, defended, agreeable face,
but the true, narrow, unrepeatable you,
in the handful of words that the cutting left through.

— *What the Edit Leaves In*, MK Parrish
