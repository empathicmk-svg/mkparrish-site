---
title: Marginalia
subtitle: On the notes in the margin, the truest things written sideways, and why the best of us lives in the parts that weren't meant to be seen.
category: Poetry
recommended_access: FREE (public — emotional anchor; the namesake poem of the publication)
source: original — The Margins

The truest thing I ever wrote
was sideways, in a margin, as a note —

not in the body, careful and revised,
but in the white space, where I improvised,

where no one graded and no one would see,
so the only one I answered to was me.

That's where the real lines tend to hide:
not center-page, but just outside —

the aside, the arrow, the second-thought star,
the *wait — this is what it's actually about* scrawled far

from the part I'd planned, the part I'd rehearsed,
the polished paragraph I'd put first.

They tell you the margin is where you're wrong.
I think it's where you've been right all along —

the place you talk to yourself, unafraid,
before the editing, before the trade

of the strange and the true for the smooth and the clean.
The margin is the realest thing I mean.

So I stopped apologizing for the edge.
I started reading my own marginalia like a pledge:

*keep the note you wrote when no one was near.
The center is the performance. The margin is here.*

And the woman I am is mostly made
of the things I muttered, unafraid,

in the white space, in my own hand, off to the side —
the marginalia I learned to stop trying to hide.

— *Marginalia*, MK Parrish
