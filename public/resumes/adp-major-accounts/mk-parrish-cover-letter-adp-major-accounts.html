Long Island's mid-market is outgrowing its payroll.

Dear ADP Major Accounts Hiring Team,

Somewhere between 50 and 159 employees, a company's payroll and HR stop being tasks and become risks: more locations, more overtime rules, more turnover, and one office manager holding it together. That's the moment ADP Workforce Now is built for, and I'd like to be the one who gets there first.

I'm applying for the Sales Representative, Major Accounts (Mid-Market) role in Melville. I run the full sales cycle today at Mercedes-Benz of Smithtown, from prospecting through financing, negotiation and close, selling a technology-heavy product on its value rather than its price. I also opened a new channel there by calling service customers before their appointments, the same instinct I'd bring to cross-selling ADP's existing clients.

Before that I spent nearly two decades selling to executives. At Ericsson, selling a cloud communications platform through partners, my deal reviews raised enterprise win rates 15% and cut sales cycles 30% on the way to $200M+ in influenced revenue. At DEFY Media I closed multimillion-dollar partnerships with Sony, Warner Bros. and Google. At Mercer I worked with CHROs and Fortune 50 HR leaders, so I know how the HR buyer thinks.

How I'd work the territory:

  • Start where I'm trusted. Through the Long Island Builders Institute I'm working with builders and subcontractors: mid-sized employers with multi-site crews, union and prevailing-wage payroll, and constant hiring.
  • Hunt with a system. AI-driven account research and multi-channel outreach (cold reply rates as high as 32%), CPA and broker referrals, and every touch in Salesforce.
  • Sell outcomes, then the platform. Open with the owner's hours, risk and turnover, then show how payroll, HR, time, benefits and talent work in one system.

I live and sell on Long Island, and I'm ready to build this territory. I'd welcome 20 minutes to walk you through how I'd start.

Thank you,
