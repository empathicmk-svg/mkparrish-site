The Resume Rewrite Kit

Turn a quiet resume into a clear, specific case for why you belong in the room.

By MK Parrish · $18

Why this exists

Most resumes are accurate and still not convincing.

They list the job. They list the tools. They say "managed," "supported," "collaborated," and "responsible for" until every strong person sounds interchangeable with every other strong person. The work may be excellent. The page is not carrying it.

This kit helps you rewrite the resume like a case. Not a biography. Not a keyword dump. A case.

Your resume is not the archive of everything you did. It is the argument for why the next room should open.

Use this when the experience is there but the language is not. Work in order. The audit finds the proof. The bank gives you raw material. The formulas turn it into bullets. The final checklist makes it clean enough for a human and structured enough for a system.

Part 1 - The Resume Positioning Audit

Before you rewrite a line, name the job the resume is trying to win.

TARGET ROLE:

  The role I want next: ______________________________


  The level/seniority I am aiming for: ________________


  The industry or company type I want: ________________


  The three skills this role must believe I have:


    1. __________________


    2. __________________


    3. __________________



MY EDGE:


  The problem I am unusually good at solving: __________


  The kind of team or environment where I do my best work:


  ______________________________________________________


  The result people can trust me to create: ____________

The one-sentence position

Fill this in before editing the resume.

I am a [ROLE / IDENTITY] who helps [TEAM / COMPANY / CUSTOMER]

achieve [OUTCOME] by [DISTINCT STRENGTH / APPROACH].

Examples:

  • I am an operations leader who helps growing teams turn messy processes into systems people actually use.
  • I am a product marketer who turns customer insight into launches that sales teams can explain.
  • I am a creative project manager who keeps ambitious work moving without making everyone live in chaos.

That sentence does not have to appear on the resume. It is the ruler. Every bullet should support it.

Part 2 - The Achievement Bank

A weak resume usually means the proof is still in your head. Pull it out before you format anything.

For each role, answer these prompts.

ROLE:

  What was broken, unclear, slow, expensive, or risky when I arrived?



  What did I build, fix, lead, write, launch, save, clean up, or improve?



  Who depended on the work?



  What changed because I did it?



  What number, name, timeline, scale, audience, or scope proves it?



  What did I do that someone else might not have done?

Proof types to hunt for

  • Revenue created or protected
  • Costs reduced
  • Time saved
  • Error rate reduced
  • Team size, budget, project scope, or audience size
  • Launches shipped
  • Systems created
  • Stakeholders aligned
  • Customers served
  • Risk removed
  • Quality improved
  • Process adopted by other teams

If you do not have a number, use scope. If you do not have scope, use stakes. If you do not have stakes, name the before and after.

Weak proof: "Managed social media."

Better proof: "Owned weekly content calendar across Instagram, LinkedIn, and email for a 12-person team, turning scattered requests into a repeatable publishing system."

Best proof: "Built a weekly content system across Instagram, LinkedIn, and email that cut last-minute requests by 40% and gave a 12-person team one source of truth."

Part 3 - The Bullet Formula

Most resume bullets are too small because they stop at the task. Use this instead:

ACTION + SCOPE + OUTCOME

Or:

BUILT / LED / FIXED [THING] for [WHO / WHAT SCALE],

resulting in [OUTCOME / PROOF].

The stronger verb list

Use verbs that show ownership and consequence:

  • Built
  • Led
  • Rebuilt
  • Launched
  • Designed
  • Simplified
  • Translated
  • Increased
  • Reduced
  • Protected
  • Consolidated
  • Operationalized
  • Turned
  • Created
  • Drove
  • Clarified
  • Shipped
  • Negotiated
  • Resolved
  • Introduced

Avoid starting every bullet with "Responsible for." Responsibility is not proof. Change is proof.

Before and after examples

Before: Responsible for onboarding new hires.

After: Rebuilt new-hire onboarding into a 30-day checklist and manager handoff system, cutting repeated training questions and helping new teammates ramp faster.

Before: Managed client communications.

After: Owned client communication across 18 active accounts, translating project updates into clear next steps and reducing status-call churn.

Before: Helped with marketing campaigns.

After: Supported three product campaigns from brief to launch, coordinating copy, creative, and reporting so the team shipped on deadline.

Before: Created reports for leadership.

After: Built weekly leadership reporting that surfaced risk, blockers, and revenue movement in one page instead of five disconnected spreadsheets.

Part 4 - The Summary Rewrite

Your summary is not a paragraph about being hard-working. It is a fast orientation for the reader.

Use this structure:

[ROLE / IDENTITY] with [YEARS OR DEPTH] in [FIELD / CONTEXT].

Known for [DISTINCT STRENGTH] and [SPECIFIC OUTCOME].


Strongest in [ENVIRONMENT / PROBLEM TYPE].

Examples:

Operations manager with 7+ years in fast-moving startup and agency environments.

Known for turning unclear processes into systems teams actually follow.


Strongest where growth has created too much manual work and nobody owns the cleanup yet.



Product marketing lead with a background in customer research, launch strategy,

and sales enablement. Known for translating complex products into language buyers


understand. Strongest in B2B teams where the product is better than the story


around it.

Keep it under five lines. The point is not to explain your whole life. The point is to make the rest of the page easier to read.

Part 5 - The Experience Section

Each role should do three things:

  1. Show what kind of problem you handled.
  2. Show the scope of your ownership.
  3. Show what changed.

Use this layout:

ROLE TITLE

Company | Location or Remote | Dates



One-line context: what the company/team did, what you owned, or why the role mattered.



  • Bullet 1: biggest outcome
  • Bullet 2: strongest scope
  • Bullet 3: proof of leadership, systems, collaboration, or judgment
  • Bullet 4: optional technical/tool/process detail if relevant

The one-line context

Add this only when the company or role is not self-explanatory.

Examples:

  • Joined a 40-person SaaS company during a sales-led growth push and owned lifecycle campaign operations.
  • Supported a founder-led creative studio with project management, client communication, and production workflows.
  • Led operations for a regional nonprofit serving 3,000+ families annually.

Context helps the reader understand why the bullets matter.

Part 6 - The Skills Section

A skills section should help search and scanning. It should not become a junk drawer.

Group skills by use:

Strategy: Positioning, customer research, go-to-market planning, messaging

Operations: Process design, onboarding, reporting, vendor coordination


Tools: HubSpot, Airtable, Notion, Asana, Google Analytics, Figma

Only include a tool if you can speak to how you used it. A resume is not a wish list.

Part 7 - The Final Polish Checklist

Run this before sending.

  • [ ] The top third of the page makes the target role obvious.
  • [ ] Every bullet starts with a strong verb.
  • [ ] No bullet is only a task. Each one includes scope, outcome, or stakes.
  • [ ] The strongest proof appears first under each role.
  • [ ] The summary is under five lines.
  • [ ] The skills section is grouped, not dumped.
  • [ ] Dates, titles, punctuation, and spacing are consistent.
  • [ ] There are no orphan bullets with one or two words on a final line.
  • [ ] The file name is clean: First-Last-Resume.pdf.
  • [ ] The resume can be skimmed in ten seconds and still tell a clear story.

The 20-Minute Rewrite Sprint

If you are short on time, do this.

  1. Write the one-sentence position.
  2. Pick your three strongest achievements.
  3. Rewrite the top three bullets using ACTION + SCOPE + OUTCOME.
  4. Rewrite the summary so it points toward the role you want.
  5. Cut every "responsible for."
  6. Save as PDF.

That is not the final version. It is the version that stops actively underselling you.
