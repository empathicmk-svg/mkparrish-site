The Bid Model

There is no CPA before there is a product. So we bid on probability.

Every pre-launch campaign that fails does so the same way: it optimizes to cheap leads and discovers on launch day that it bought an email list, not an order book. The fix is to stop treating a lead as one thing. We grade every lead by observed commitment, attach a modeled probability of funding, and derive a separate bid cap per grade — so the auction can never buy us volume we can't convert.

Max Bidgrade = Target CAC × P(fund | grade)  where  Target CAC = (LTV × GM) ÷ LTV:CAC = ($310 × 0.78) ÷ 3.0 = $80.60

Three Budgets · Zero-Based, Not Inherited

Every dollar defends itself against a bid cap.

These are zero-based allocations — nothing is funded because it was funded last time. Each channel earns budget only if its modeled cost per Grade-A/B lead clears the bid-model ladder. Note how the mix shifts away from auction media as budget grows: pre-launch, incremental auction dollars hit saturation fast because the in-market audience is finite, so scale buys guaranteed and owned inventory instead.

Five budgeting methods most launch plans don't use CFO TOOLKIT

  • Upfront vs. scatter arbitrage. Podcast, newsletter and CTV inventory bought as PG/flat-fee in T-6–T-5 locks pre-Q4 rates. The same impressions bought in scatter in late September cost 25–40% more. Modeled saving: $47K on the Base plan.
  • Working-media ratio as a covenant. Non-working spend (production, fees, tooling) capped at 18%. Any breach is a CFO-Gate conversation, not a line-item surprise.
  • Barter / VIK and contra deals. Slate talent and their existing brand partners hold co-op budgets and audiences. Audience swaps and contra placements are media at zero cash cost — target 8–12% of total delivered impressions.
  • Reserve laddering. 6% held unallocated until T-2, then released only into channels that have proven a Grade-A cost below cap. Prevents the classic error of committing the full budget before any evidence exists.
  • Make-goods written into every guaranteed buy. If a publisher under-delivers viewable impressions or a creator misses posting windows, we take value back in inventory — protects the plan from partner risk without a lawyer.
  • Capital-efficiency framing for the board. Pre-launch spend is reported as cost per $1,000 of indicated capital ($81 on the Base plan), not as marketing expense. It reframes the flight as a capital-formation activity, which is what it actually is.

The cross-promotion thesis — why this is IPX's structural advantage

Most launches buy audiences because they have none. IPX is different: it has signed 100+ celebrity, athlete, and influencer projects, each with an audience and each with an economic interest in the platform succeeding. That is a distribution network that competitors would have to rent and IPX simply owns by contract.

The operational move is unglamorous and worth more than any media buy: put promotional obligations into the listing agreement — minimum post counts across the pre-launch window, raw-footage rights, and cross-tagging requirements. Partnerships owns the paper; media owns the calendar. If this is executed well, roughly 8–12% of delivered impressions carry zero cash cost and outperform paid social on Grade-A yield, because they arrive as endorsement rather than advertising.

The Agent Layer · MCP + LLM Automation

A media team of six that never sleeps.

Each agent is an MCP-connected LLM service with a narrow job, a numeric trigger, and a human approval gate on anything touching money. Modeled impact: −18% blended CPQL and ~34 analyst-hours/week returned to strategy.

PACER

Bid & pacing agent. Reads hourly spend and cost-per-grade via ad-platform MCP servers; enforces the bid caps automatically within a ±15% band. Anything larger drafts the change and pings Slack for one-tap approval. Guardrail: hard daily cap per channel.

GRADER

Lead-quality scorer. Writes lead grade back to every platform as a custom conversion within 90 seconds of capture, so algorithms optimize toward reservations rather than emails. The single highest-leverage automation in the stack.

FATIGUE

Creative-fatigue sentinel. Watches frequency and 3-day CTR decay per asset; flags at −25%, queues the next approved variant, drafts new hooks from winning transcripts. Modeled: −18% CPQL vs. static rotation.

SCOUT

Influencer discovery crawler. Scores TikTok/YouTube candidates against the creator-roster rubric via audience APIs; ships a ranked top-20 to Thursday's Creative Council. 200+ profiles/wk screened; humans pick from 20.

LISTEN

Sentiment & community listener. Streams comments, Reddit and X; classifies trust-blocking objections ("is this a scam?", "what happens if IPX folds?") and feeds the top three weekly into creative and FAQ. Fires the War-Room webhook past threshold. The crisis tripwire for the War Room.

COMPLY

Compliance pre-check. Every script and ad passes an LLM tuned on legal's rules — no performance promises, required disclosures, no pre-effective offer language — before human review. Legal SLA 48h → 12h.

The War Room · Three Pre-Launch Failure Modes

Plans are tested by the weeks that go wrong.

Three failure modes rehearsed in advance, each with a tripwire, a decision tree and a re-entry condition — because the difference between a bad week and a failed launch is how much of the response was pre-authorized.

T-4: CPQL looks great at $19 — but Grade A mix has collapsed to 6% SEV-1 · QUALITY

WHY THIS IS THE MOST DANGEROUS SCENARIO

  • It looks like success. Volume up, cost down, dashboard green — and the order book is filling with people who will never fund. This is how pre-launch campaigns fail silently and only reveal it on launch day, when nothing converts.
  • Diagnose: decompose CPQL by grade and source. Modeled finding — one prospecting audience delivering Grade C at $9 is flattering the blend while Grade A quietly costs $140.
  • Second check: is it the ad or the offer? If Grade C→B step-up is broken, the reservation flow is the problem, not the media.

RESPOND WITH NUMBERS

  • Re-point every campaign objective from registration to the reservation event — GRADER already writes it back; the fix is bidding to it, and it costs nothing but a week of relearning.
  • Kill the Grade-C-only audiences outright. Modeled effect: blended CPQL rises to $31 and that is the correct direction — we're paying for probability, not volume.
  • Add a friction step that qualifies rather than deters: a deposit-intent question. Lower volume, dramatically better grade mix.
  • Re-entry rule: resume scaled prospecting when Grade A mix ≥18% for 5 consecutive days.

T-2: legal signals the Sep 18 market open slips to mid-October SEV-1 · TIMELINE

THE PRE-LAUNCH NIGHTMARE

  • Six weeks of urgency creative point at a date that no longer exists. Worse, the order book is a decaying asset — at 4%/week, a 4-week slip costs ~15% of modeled indicated capital with zero media waste to blame.
  • The instinct is to hide it. That's the one fatal option — nothing destroys a financial platform's credibility faster than a silently missed date that early believers discover themselves.
  • Immediate freeze on all date-specific creative and countdown assets within 4 hours; COMPLY sweeps live copy for "Sep 18" references across every channel and partner.

CONVERT THE DELAY INTO AN ASSET

  • Tell the list first, and frame it correctly: a regulated exchange delaying for compliance is evidence the compliance is real. Founder video to reserved members within 24h, before any public statement.
  • Pay the believers for their patience: extend Founding Member first-look pricing, add an allocation bonus for reservations already placed. Converts a broken promise into a loyalty event and defends the order book against decay.
  • Cut paid spend 55% into a maintenance flight — retargeting and content only. Preserve the audience, stop buying urgency you can't honor. Modeled saving redeployed to the new T-2/T-1: $96K.
  • Re-entry: full spend resumes at new-date T-3 with the reserved cohort as the seed audience — warmer and cheaper than the original build.

T-5: Meta and Google reject creative — unregistered securities / financial-product policy SEV-2 · CHANNEL

EXPECTED, NOT SURPRISING

  • Pre-launch is the hardest advertising window for a securities platform: you cannot make offer claims before effectiveness, and platform reviewers apply blunt rules to nuanced products. We plan for rejection rather than appeal it in a panic.
  • Which is precisely why no channel exceeds 40% of pre-launch spend, and why flat-fee native was locked in T-6 — the hedge was bought before it was needed.
  • Certification package (entity docs, disclaimers, compliant landing experience) pre-assembled in COMPLY; filed within 4 hours, rep escalation same day.

REROUTE AND REFRAME

  • Change the creative, not just the appeal: run content and category education ("how fractional IP ownership works") rather than offer claims. Passes review, and pre-launch it converts better anyway — nobody is ready to commit on first exposure.
  • Shift ~$9K/day (Base) into channels with no securities policy gate: newsletters, podcasts, Reddit, creator whitelisting, PMP/CTV. All were bought flat and can absorb it immediately.
  • Modeled damage: blended CPQL drifts $26 → $30 for the affected fortnight — inside tolerance, no panic cuts.
  • Structural fix banked: registered backup ad accounts and a pre-cleared creative library for the post-launch flight, when offer language finally becomes permissible.
CFO Lens · The Variables That Move Pre-Launch Math

The spreadsheet lives inside a market.

Six variables priced into this plan — including the ones that don't look like marketing problems until they've already cost you the launch.

Intent decay — the hidden one

A lead is a depreciating asset. Modeled at 4%/week, a T-6 lead converts at ~0.78× a T-1 lead. This single variable justifies the entire back-loaded pacing curve — and it's why "start early and coast" is the most expensive pre-launch mistake. It also means any launch delay has a calculable cost, not a vague one.

Q4 upfront vs. scatter

Holiday advertisers and the Nov 3 midterms stack into Q4 auctions; CPMs inflate 25–40%. Our flight ends Sep 17, mostly ahead of it — but the post-launch flight lands inside it. Lock PG/PMP and flat-fee native now, at pre-launch rates, for delivery after launch. Modeled saving: $47K.

Labor Day inventory dip

Monday Sep 7 sits inside T-2. Auction CPMs typically soften over the long weekend as brand advertisers pause. We deliberately lean in — cheap reach against an audience that is home, on-device, and reachable, three days before the commitment push.

Risk-appetite beta

Alt-asset commitment correlates with risk-on sentiment. A >15% weekly BTC drawdown should be expected to cut reservation rate 3–5 pts. The plan shifts message weight from yield to ownership automatically — culture-framed creative decays less with risk mood, and we hold both versions live to make the swap instant.

Two-sided book risk

An exchange needs supply and demand at open. All the investor demand in the world fails if listings aren't ready. Media therefore spends against rights-holders and managers (industry events, trades) as well as investors — the only line item where marketing is de-risking the product, not just selling it.

Creator-risk concentration

A hero partner's controversy is a balance-sheet risk to the flight. Cap any single creator at 8% of budget, morals clause and pause rights in every contract, and SCOUT holds two vetted understudies warm per tier. Concentration risk is managed the way a portfolio manages it — with limits, set in advance.
