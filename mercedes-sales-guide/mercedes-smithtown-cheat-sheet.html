02

Decode the Badge

A customer will test you: "What's the difference between a C 300 and a C 43?" Read the trunk lid like a sentence — Class letter · number · powertrain · driveline — and you'll always have the answer.

The Number
Historically engine displacement; today it's a position on the ladder. Higher number = more power/output within that class. A GLE 450 sits above a GLE 350.
4MATIC
Mercedes' all-wheel drive. On Long Island this is the default expectation for snow. "4MATIC+" is the performance AWD on AMG cars that can shift power fully to the rear.
AMG
The performance division (Affalterbach, Germany — "one man, one engine"). AMG 35/43/45/53/63 = increasing performance. A 63 is the top dog. AMG is a mindset sale: sound, launch, exclusivity.
Maybach
The ultra-luxury sub-brand above the S-Class and GLS. Sell the rear seat, the ride, the two-tone paint, the champagne flutes — not the horsepower.
EQ
The electric family (EQB, EQE, EQS). "EQ Technology" now also brands the newest electric models like the G 580. "EQ Boost" was the older 48-volt mild-hybrid.
Body Letters
GL_ = SUV (GLA, GLC, GLE, GLS). CL_ = 4-door coupe/coupe styling (CLA, CLE). SL = roadster. Add the class letter after GL to place its size.
4/6/8/PHEV
Know what's under the hood: turbo 4-cyl (C/E 300-class, GLC), inline 6 (the 450s, silky-smooth, mild-hybrid), V8 (580s & big AMGs), and plug-in hybrids (the new C 63 & E 53 are electrified performance).
The "S" suffix
On AMG (e.g., GLE 63 S) it means the hotter, higher-output version. Don't confuse with S-Class.
⚑ Know this objection cold

"The new AMG C 63 is only a 4-cylinder?" — Yes, and it makes 671 hp as an F1-derived plug-in hybrid, more than the old V8. Frame it as Formula 1 technology on the street, not "a downgrade." Same script for the E 53 Hybrid.

Trims & Packages

Packages are where gross lives and where you differentiate two cars that "look the same." Mercedes bundles most modern models into three trim tiers, then layers optional packages on top.

The three trim tiers (C, E & most new models)

Premium base equipped

  • KEYLESS-GO push-button start
  • Blind-spot assist, heated front seats
  • The volume lease build

Exclusive mid

  • Everything in Premium plus
  • Burmester audio, 360° camera
  • Ventilated seats, memory, ambient upgrade

Pinnacle top

  • Everything in Exclusive plus
  • Head-Up Display, augmented-reality nav
  • MBUX rear tablet / full tech

The packages that actually sell

AMG Line
The look without the AMG engine — sportier bumpers, wheels, flat-bottom wheel. Huge on leases; customers want the aggressive face.
Night Package
Blacked-out exterior trim, mirrors, badges. Pairs with AMG Line. The single most-requested style add.
Driver Assistance Pkg
The big safety upgrade: DISTRONIC adaptive cruise, Active Steering Assist, active lane change, PRE-SAFE. The "it almost drives itself" package.
Parking Assistance Pkg
360° surround camera + active park assist. Essential talking point for tight LI garages and parallel parking.
Warmth & Comfort
Heated steering wheel, heated armrests, rapid heat. An easy winter close on Long Island.
Acoustic Comfort
Thicker laminated glass — quieter cabin. Demo it: roll the windows up on the test drive and let them hear the silence.
Burmester® Sound
Premium (and 3D High-End on flagships) audio. Always cue up their music on the demo, not yours.
Panorama Roof
Fixed or opening glass roof. Opens up the cabin; walk them to it before they sit down.

Tech & Feature Glossary

These are the trademarked names customers hear in ads and from friends. Say them fluently and you sound like an expert; fumble them and you sound like a green pea.

MBUX
Mercedes-Benz User Experience — the infotainment brain. Wake it with "Hey Mercedes." Demo the voice command every single time.
Hyperscreen
The giant single glass panel spanning the dash (EQS, S-Class option). The showroom "wow." Superscreen is the passenger-display version.
DISTRONIC
Adaptive cruise control that keeps distance and can stop/go in traffic. The core of Driver Assistance.
AIRMATIC
Air suspension — adjustable ride height & comfort. Standard on flagships, optional up-sell elsewhere.
E-ACTIVE BODY CONTROL
Predictive suspension that scans the road and leans into curves. S/GLE flagship party trick.
PRE-SAFE®
Pre-crash system — tightens belts, closes windows, plays a sound to protect hearing. A safety-buyer's dream feature.
MULTIBEAM / DIGITAL LIGHT
Adaptive LED headlights; DIGITAL LIGHT can project symbols onto the road. Great night-drive demo.
DYNAMIC SELECT
Drive modes — Comfort, Sport, Sport+, Eco, Individual. Let them feel Sport vs Comfort on the test drive.
4MATIC / 4MATIC+
AWD / performance AWD (see the badge section). LI snow answer.
Mercedes me
The connected-car app — remote start, lock, vehicle status. Set it up at delivery; it drives CSI and retention.
ENERGIZING Comfort
Wellness program syncing lighting, climate, massage, scent. Demo it on a stressed shopper — it's magic.
05

Sales-Floor Slang

The language the desk, your manager, and the vets speak. Nod along on day one; use it correctly by week two.

Up / Ups
A prospect who walks in. "It's your up" = your turn with the next customer.
Be-back
Customer who leaves promising to "come back." The industry joke: be-backs rarely come back — so close today.
Green pea
A brand-new salesperson. That's you — wear it with hustle, not shame.
The Desk
The sales manager who structures/approves deals. You "go to the desk" for numbers.
T.O. (Turn Over)
Handing the customer to a manager/closer when you're stuck. Not failure — it's a tool.
Four-square
The worksheet: trade, price, down payment, monthly. The classic negotiation grid.
F&I
Finance & Insurance office — where the deal is finalized and back-end products are sold.
Front / Back gross
Profit on the car itself (front) vs. financing & products (back). Your pay lives in both.
Mini
A minimum-commission deal (little/no gross). Better than a lost sale — it counts toward your 12.
Spiff
A bonus for moving a specific unit (aged inventory, a slow model). Ask what's spiffed.
Demo
A dealer car you drive; also the act of demonstrating a vehicle to a customer.
Lot rot / Aged
Units sitting too long. Move these — managers love it and they're often spiffed.
ACV
Actual Cash Value — what the dealer really values a trade at (vs. the "allowance" the customer sees).
Upside down
Customer owes more than the trade is worth (negative equity). Roll it or restructure.
Skate
Stealing another salesperson's customer. Never do this. Fastest way to lose the floor's trust.
CSI
Customer Satisfaction Index — the survey score. Mercedes lives and dies by it; your bonuses may too.
PDI
Pre-Delivery Inspection — prepping the car before delivery. Confirm it's done before you promise a time.

Leasing 101 — Your #1 Skill

The majority of luxury Mercedes move on lease. Master this and you'll out-sell veterans who only think in purchase price. You don't set the numbers — the desk does — but you must speak them.

Cap Cost
The "selling price" of the car for the lease. Lower it and the payment drops. Capitalized Cost Reduction = money down.
Residual Value
What the car is projected to be worth at lease-end (set by MB Financial). Higher residual = lower payment. Mercedes residuals are strong — a selling point.
Money Factor (MF)
The lease "interest rate." Multiply by 2,400 to get the rough APR. A .00125 MF ≈ 3% APR.
Depreciation
Cap cost − residual. You're financing the drop in value, not the whole car — why leases are cheaper monthly.
Acquisition Fee
Bank fee to start the lease (rolled in or paid up front). Know it's coming so it doesn't surprise the customer.
Disposition Fee
Fee at lease-end if they walk away/don't re-lease. Waived when they get another Benz — a retention hook.
Mileage Allowance
Usually 7,500 / 10,000 / 12,000 mi/yr. Overage is billed per mile. Qualify their commute early.
MSD
Multiple Security Deposits — an MB program where refundable deposits lower the money factor. Advanced move; ask the desk.
Why lease — the script

"With a lease you're only paying for the years you actually drive it, so you stay in a newer, safer, fully-covered Mercedes for a lower monthly payment — and you get a brand-new one every three years instead of worrying about what a six-year-old car is worth."

The Road to the Sale

Every deal follows the same steps. Skip a step and you lose control. Follow the road and the close is just the natural last stop.

1

Meet & Greet

Warm, low-pressure, hand out a card. Get the name and use it.

Say: "Welcome to Mercedes-Benz of Smithtown — are you here to look at anything specific, or just enjoying some new cars today?"

2

Qualify / Interview

Needs before numbers. Budget, commute (miles!), who drives it, own vs. lease, timeline, trade.

Say: "So I put you in exactly the right one — what's this car mainly going to do for you?"

3

Select the Vehicle

Pick two: one that fits the budget, one aspirational. Never overwhelm with the whole lot.

4

Walkaround / Present

Feature → benefit → them. "This is the 360 camera (feature), so you'll never curb a wheel (benefit), which matters with your tight garage (them)."

5

Test Drive

The car sells itself here. Their music on, phone paired, Sport mode once. Let silence do the work.

6

Trial Close

Temperature check before numbers. "Can you see yourself in this one?" / "What color would you want?"

7

Write-Up / Desk

Get commitment to sit down. Present payments (lease + finance + cash lanes). Go to the desk with a real customer, not a tire-kicker.

8

Close & Handle Objections

Isolate the one real objection, answer it, ask for the sale. Silence after you ask — whoever speaks first loses.

9

F&I Hand-Off

Walk them in warm, set up the finance manager to win. Never bad-mouth the back-end products.

10

Delivery & Follow-Up

Set up Mercedes me, pair the phone, take the photo. Then follow up day 1, day 7, day 30 — this is where referrals & your 12s next month come from.

08

Objection Handling

An objection is a request for more information, not a "no." Acknowledge, answer, ask again. Keep these responses in your back pocket.

They sayYou say
"I need to think about it.""Totally fair — usually when someone says that, it's the money, the car, or the timing. Which one is it, so I can actually help?"
"The payment's too high."Isolate: term, money down, or the car? Re-work the structure (lease, term, mileage) — don't just drop price. "If we could get you comfortable on the monthly, is this the car?"
"I want to shop around.""Smart — most people do. What matters is you get the right car at the right number today; let me make it easy so there's nothing left to shop for."
"I can get it cheaper elsewhere."Don't panic or trash them. "I believe you — let's make sure it's the same car, same term, same miles. Apples to apples, I'll earn your business."
"I need to talk to my spouse."Should've been qualified in step 2. "Absolutely — let's get them on the phone / when can you both come in? What do you think they'll want to know?"
"Just give me your best price.""You'll get a great number — first let me make sure this is even the right car for you, or a great price on the wrong car helps no one."
"It's only a 4-cylinder.""F1-derived turbo — the AMG C 63 makes 671 hp, more than the old V8, and it's a plug-in hybrid so you get silent EV around town. It's an upgrade, not a compromise."
"I'll wait for the new model.""The current one is fully available today with strong residuals — the moment the redesign lands, your lease-end timing lets you jump right into it. Let's start the clock now."
⚑ The three closes to master first

Assumptive: "I'll get the paperwork started" (act as if it's done).   Trial: "If I could do X, would you take delivery today?"   Alternative: "Do you prefer the black or the silver?" — either answer is a yes.

Cross-Shop — Know the Competition

On Long Island your customer is also visiting BMW, Audi, Lexus, Genesis, and Tesla. Never trash a rival — reframe. Sell the Mercedes difference: presence, comfort, safety heritage, resale.

vs. BMW Competition BMW down the road

  • BMW = "sport." We = presence + comfort + luxury.
  • Sell the quieter cabin, the ride, the badge status.
  • "BMW is a great driver's car — Mercedes is the car you want to live in every day."

vs. Audi tech-forward

  • Audi = clean tech. We = MBUX + heritage + resale.
  • Lean on brand prestige and stronger lease residuals.

vs. Lexus reliability play

  • Lexus = reliable/quiet. We = the same refinement with real status.
  • "You'll get the comfort you love and a badge that says more."

vs. Genesis value challenger

  • Genesis = value + warranty. We = the star, resale, dealer network.
  • Acknowledge the value, sell the brand equity & lease math.

vs. Tesla EV shopper

  • Point them to EQE / EQS. Sell fit-and-finish, service, real interior luxury.
  • "Same electric thrill, wrapped in a Mercedes — and a dealership that takes care of you."

Universal reframe use always

  • "They all make a nice car. The question is which one is right for you — let's find that, not just the cheapest."

The 12-Car Month Game Plan

12 units in month one is aggressive but absolutely doable — it's math and activity, not luck. Work backward from the goal and control the inputs.

→ Ups needed to hit 12~60

Daily non-negotiables
  • Be first, leave last. More floor time = more ups.
  • Take every up — greenies win on hustle, not skill.
  • Work your sphere: 20 calls/texts to friends, family, past contacts. Everyone knows you sell Benz now.
  • Follow up every be-back and internet lead within minutes.
  • Know 3 aged/spiffed units cold — easy grosses & manager love.
  • Log everything in the CRM. No lead falls through.
Where your 12 will come from
  • Floor ups — walk-in traffic (your bread & butter).
  • Internet / BDC leads — speed wins; call in seconds.
  • Lease renewals / equity mining — the desk can pull lists of customers you can put in a new car cheaper.
  • Sphere of influence — your first 2–3 deals are often people who know you.
  • Referrals — deliver an amazing experience, ask for two names every time.
  • Service drive — customers getting service are shopping. Walk it daily.
The green-pea mindset

You will not out-knowledge the 20-year vet in month one — so out-work, out-follow-up, and out-care them. Product knowledge (this sheet) makes you credible. Activity makes you money. Do the reps, take the T.O. when stuck, protect your CSI, and ask for the sale every single time.
