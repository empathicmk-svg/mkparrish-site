## The Girl Who Found Her Words

In a kingdom that loved a good story there lived a girl named Willa, whose words took their time.

It was a kingdom built on stories. Every year it held the Great Telling — a festival where, one by one, the people stood in the square and told a story, and the whole kingdom listened. To tell your story well was the proudest thing a person could do.

Willa loved stories more than anyone. Her head was full of them. But when she tried to say them out loud, her words took their time. The first sound would catch, and c-c-catch, and stick behind her teeth, and she would wait, and try again, gently, until it came loose.

And the kingdom, which loved a good story, was not very good at waiting. People finished her sentences for her. They looked away. Some got a pained, hurrying look, as if her slowness were a splinter they wished someone would pull out.

So Willa did the saddest thing a girl full of stories can do. She stopped talking. She kept her stories inside, where they were safe, where nobody could rush them. And everyone decided Willa simply had nothing to say.

They were so wrong. Willa had *everything* to say. She just needed a kingdom patient enough to hear it.

Then a strange trouble came. At the Great Telling, the first teller stood to begin — and could not remember her story. Nor could the next. Nor the one after. A gray forgetting had settled over the kingdom. The fast talkers had told their tales so quickly, so carelessly, for so many years, that they had never really *held* them. And now the stories had slipped right through their rushing fingers.

The kingdom was in despair. A Great Telling with nothing to tell! The most wonderful day of the year — ruined.

And at the very back, a quiet voice said, "I remember one."

Willa's big brother heard. He leapt onto a bench and shouted, "My sister remembers a story! But you have to *let her tell it.* All the way. However long it takes."

The square went quiet. Every face turned to Willa.

So Willa stood up. And she began. The first word caught, and c-c-caught, and stuck behind her teeth. And here is the thing that had never once happened in all of Willa's life: *nobody rushed her.* Nobody finished it for her. The whole kingdom simply waited — quiet and still and leaning in — and let the word come loose in its own good time. And it did. And then the next one.

And Willa began to tell the most beautiful story the kingdom had ever heard. Because here was the secret nobody had guessed: all those years she wasn't talking, she had been *keeping.* Holding her stories carefully, learning them by heart in a way the fast talkers never had to. Her words took their time — so every one of them had been chosen, and polished, and *meant.* She did not have less to say. She had more. She had been saving it.

When she finished, the square was so quiet you could hear the wind. And then the whole kingdom rose to its feet and roared — the loudest, longest cheer in the history of the Great Telling — for the girl whose words took their time.

And every year after, the teller the whole kingdom leaned in to hear was Willa — who was never, ever rushed again.

## The End — And Your Beginning

The world is in a terrible hurry. If your words take their time — if they catch, and stick, and have to be coaxed out one careful piece at a time — the hurrying world will try to finish them for you, or decide you have nothing to say.

The hurrying world is wrong.

Your voice is not too slow. It is careful. It is chosen. It is worth waiting for. So take all the time you need. Let the word catch, and try again, gently, the way Willa did. The right people — the ones who love you — will lean in and wait.

The kingdom is leaning in. Take your time. We're listening.
