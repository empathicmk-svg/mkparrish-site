## The House Style

Every carousel uses the same visual system. Consistency is the asset — when your slides look the same each week, the look itself becomes recognizable, and recognition is the beginning of authority.

- **1080 × 1080 square pages** — the native LinkedIn carousel ratio.
- **Near-black editorial background** with a restrained blush-pink glow. No bright white decks.
- **Your name top left, page count top right.** Quiet, consistent, unmistakable.
- **Bebas Neue uppercase headlines. DM Sans body. Playfair Display italic signature.**
- **Pearl white, smoke grey, and one petal pink** — used only on the word carrying the tension.
- **Left-aligned copy, generous negative space.** White space reads as confidence.
- **A pink `SWIPE →` block bottom left**, your handle bottom right.
- **No** rounded cards, stock icons, overpowering gradients, or generic Canva decoration.

The rule under all of it: nothing decorative earns its place. If an element is not carrying the idea, it is in the way.

## The Seven-Slide Story

The default carousel is a seven-slide arc. The slide count can flex when the idea demands it, but the rhythm stays the same — tension, then proof, then a rule, then one move.

1. **Cover** — a strong promise with sharp tension. The whole swipe is earned or lost here.
2. **Problem** — name what the audience is getting wrong. Make them feel seen.
3. **Evidence 01** — your first proof: a before/after, an example, a step.
4. **Evidence 02** — the second.
5. **Evidence 03** — the third. Three is the number that feels complete without dragging.
6. **Principle** — distill the lesson into one memorable rule they can repeat.
7. **CTA** — one specific next move. Not "thoughts?" — a real action.

The arc works because it mirrors how trust actually forms: you name the pain, you prove you understand it, you hand over a rule, and then — only then — you ask for something.

## Voice Rules

The look gets the swipe. The voice gets the follow.

- Clear over clever, but never bland.
- Specific outcomes instead of adjectives. "Onboarding cut from six weeks to six days," not "we drive efficiency."
- One idea per slide. If a slide has two points, it is two slides.
- Headlines sound like *you*, not a corporate content team.
- Body copy stays conversational and decisive.
- Avoid em dashes, jargon fog, and manufactured inspiration.
- Pink emphasis is a scalpel, not a highlighter — one word per slide, the word that carries the tension.

## The Formula: One Idea → One Carousel

You do not start with seven slides. You start with one sentence and pressure-test it.

**Step 1 — Find the one idea.** Finish this: *"Most people in my field believe ____, but the truth is ____."* That tension is your cover.

**Step 2 — Name the cost.** What does the wrong belief actually cost the reader — in money, time, or standing? That is slide two.

**Step 3 — Find three proofs.** The fastest, most credible proof is the before/after. Take three weak examples and rewrite each one strong. Real beats hypothetical every time.

**Step 4 — Write the rule.** Compress the whole lesson into one line short enough to screenshot. If it takes two sentences, keep cutting.

**Step 5 — Pick one move.** A checklist, a free resource, a DM prompt, a booked call. One. A carousel that asks for two things gets neither.

That is the entire method. The templates below are this formula, pre-poured into the right shape.

### Inline formatting

Inside any JSON copy field:

- `{{word}}` makes a word **petal pink** — your one emphasis per slide.
- `**phrase**` makes body copy **bold**.

That is the entire markup language. Two marks. Everything else is just writing.

## How to Build

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) installed once. After that, every carousel is one command.

1. Duplicate a template and rename it — for example `content/linkedin/carousels/my-idea.json`.
2. Write your copy into the fields. One idea per slide. Keep it tight.
3. Run the build:

```bash
npm run linkedin:carousel -- content/linkedin/carousels/my-idea.json
```

You get individual PNG slides and a LinkedIn-ready multi-page PDF in `output/linkedin/<slug>/`. Upload the PDF to LinkedIn as a document post, and the slides become a native swipeable carousel.

No design tool. No subscription. No alignment wars. The same clean deck, every time, in the time it takes to write the words.

## Ship It: The Posting Rhythm

A carousel kit is only an asset if it ships. Three rules keep it alive:

- **One carousel a week beats four in a burst.** The look compounds through repetition. Pick a day and defend it.
- **Lead with the teardown format until people know you for it.** A recognizable format is its own authority signal.
- **Put the same CTA on every cover and last slide for a month.** Repetition is how a "next move" becomes a habit in your reader.

> You will not out-discipline a blank canvas. Build the template once and let it carry the weeks your motivation doesn't show up.
