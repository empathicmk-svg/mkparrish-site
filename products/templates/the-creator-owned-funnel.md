## What This Is

Followers are borrowed attention. Email is owned attention. Offers are how attention becomes a business.

The Creator-Owned Funnel gives you the simple creator monetization path: one useful lead magnet, one welcome sequence, one low-ticket offer, one recurring channel, and one clean bridge from social content to something you control.

This is for creators who have momentum but no system behind it. People like the posts. They reply to stories. They ask for links. Then the moment passes because there is nowhere for the attention to go.

Build the place.

> The algorithm can introduce you. It should not be the only place your audience can find you.

## Section One: The Funnel Map

Use this five-part map:

1. **Discovery:** social posts, videos, carousels, collaborations.
2. **Capture:** free download, checklist, private list, challenge, quiz, or resource.
3. **Nurture:** 3-5 email welcome sequence.
4. **First sale:** low-ticket ebook, template, audit, affiliate, or mini-session.
5. **Recurring revenue:** paid newsletter, membership, retainer, sponsor, or monthly product.

If one part is missing, money leaks.

### The Simplest Version

- One pinned post
- One landing page
- One free PDF
- Three emails
- One $9-$49 product
- One monthly paid option

You do not need a complicated funnel. You need a visible next step.

## Section Two: The Lead Magnet

A lead magnet should solve a problem that happens before the paid offer.

Use this frame:

**Free thing:** helps them [quick win].
**Paid thing:** helps them [deeper or more complete result].

Examples:

- Free: 10 hooks for brand deal pitches. Paid: The Brand Deal Room.
- Free: Product fit checklist. Paid: The TikTok Shop Sprint.
- Free: Voice snapshot worksheet. Paid: The AI Content Twin.
- Free: Content bank checklist. Paid: The Repurposing Engine.

### Lead Magnet Test

Your free resource should be:

- specific
- immediately useful
- connected to a paid next step
- easy to deliver
- not so complete that nobody needs the offer

Do not make the free thing a vague newsletter promise. Give them a useful asset.

## Section Three: The Welcome Sequence

Three emails are enough to start.

### Email 1: Deliver And Orient

Subject: Here is your [resource]

Job:

- deliver the file
- remind them why they downloaded it
- name the problem
- set expectation for the next email

CTA:

- reply with their situation
- or click the relevant paid offer

### Email 2: Teach The Bigger Problem

Subject: The reason this keeps leaking

Job:

- explain why the quick win is not the whole system
- give one useful framework
- show the cost of doing nothing

CTA:

- read the product page
- book the audit
- join the paid list

### Email 3: Make The Offer

Subject: The next step

Job:

- name the paid offer
- explain who it is for
- explain what it helps them do
- make the checkout obvious

CTA:

- buy now

Do not apologize for selling. You invited them into a useful path. The offer is part of the path.

## Section Four: The Low-Ticket Ladder

Creators need a small paid product before the big ask.

Good first offers:

- $9 swipe file
- $18 checklist bundle
- $34 prompt pack
- $39 mini workbook
- $47 media kit/rate card kit
- $97 audit

The job is not to make someone rich instantly. The job is to create buyer behavior. A $39 buyer is warmer than a free subscriber.

### The Ladder

Use this:

1. Free resource
2. $9-$49 self-serve product
3. $97-$300 audit/session
4. $500-$2,500 implementation
5. recurring membership, retainer, or sponsor package

Every rung should feel like the next obvious move, not a random menu.

## Section Five: Social CTAs That Do Not Feel Desperate

Use CTAs that match the post.

For teaching posts:

- "I made the worksheet for this. It is in the shop."
- "Save this, then grab the full kit if you want the templates."
- "The complete version is linked in my bio."

For story posts:

- "If you are in this season, start with the free download."
- "I built the guide I wish I had before I figured this out."

For product/affiliate posts:

- "I linked the exact one I used."
- "Start with the checklist before you buy."

For authority posts:

- "If you want the full system, it is inside [product]."
- "This is the work I do in [service], but the self-guided version is in the shop."

The CTA should not interrupt the post. It should complete it.

## Section Six: The Weekly Operating Rhythm

One hour per week:

1. Check what content drove replies, saves, clicks, or sales.
2. Turn one strong post into an email.
3. Add one product CTA to one relevant post.
4. Improve one landing page line.
5. Follow up with one warm buyer or lead.

Creator businesses grow when attention gets handled. Not when every post starts from scratch.
