## What This Is

Repurposing is not copying and pasting the same caption everywhere. That is distribution. Repurposing is translation.

The Repurposing Engine shows you how to take one strong idea and turn it into a month of platform-native content: short videos, carousels, LinkedIn posts, email, story frames, hooks, and a paid product angle.

The point is not to create more noise. The point is to stop wasting good ideas because you only knew how to publish them once.

> One strong idea should not die after one post. It should become a small campaign.

## Section One: The Core Idea

Start with a spine sentence.

**People think [common belief], but the real truth is [your point of view], which means [practical implication].**

Examples:

- People think consistency is discipline, but the real truth is consistency is a system, which means you need a backlog before you need motivation.
- People think brand deals come from follower count, but the real truth is brands buy useful attention, which means your proof matters before your reach.
- People think AI makes content easier, but the real truth is AI amplifies unclear voice, which means the prompt is only as good as the point of view.

If the spine is weak, every derivative post gets weaker.

### The Four Tests

A core idea is worth repurposing if it has:

1. **Tension:** it argues with something.
2. **Usefulness:** it helps the audience do or see something.
3. **Repeatability:** it can become examples, steps, and stories.
4. **Commercial path:** it points toward an offer, service, affiliate, or product.

| Email/Substack | 1 | Depth and owned channel |

That is 30 pieces from one idea, but only if each piece has a different job.

## Section Three: Platform Translation

### TikTok/Reels

Use the spoken tension.

Hook:

"You do not need [common advice]. You need [real fix]."

Structure:

1. Say the tension in the first three seconds.
2. Give one example.
3. Show the practical next move.
4. End with a direct but light CTA.

### LinkedIn

Use the argument.

Structure:

1. Two-line hook.
2. Short story or observation.
3. Framework.
4. Specific takeaway.
5. CTA to comment, save, or read/buy.

### Instagram Carousel

Use the steps.

Slides:

1. Hook
2. The mistake
3. The real issue
4. Step one
5. Step two
6. Step three
7. Save/share CTA

### Email Or Substack

Use the depth.

Structure:

1. Personal context.
2. The point of view.
3. The framework.
4. A story or proof point.
5. One useful next action.
6. Soft product/service bridge.

## Section Four: The Anti-Content-Soup Rule

Repurposing fails when everything sounds like a chopped-up version of the same post.

Use this rule:

**Same spine. Different doorway.**

Doorways:

- story
- step-by-step
- mistake
- checklist
- example
- myth
- behind the scenes
- opinion
- response to a question
- before/after

If two posts have the same doorway, change one.

### Example

Core idea: creators do not need more ideas; they need a content bank.

Doorways:

- Story: "The week I stopped posting from panic."
- Checklist: "What goes inside a content bank."
- Mistake: "Why your content calendar keeps failing."
- Opinion: "Consistency is not a personality trait."
- Offer: "Download the system I use to build the bank."

Same idea. Five different ways in.

---

## Section Five: The Revenue Bridge

Every repurposed campaign should point somewhere.

Choose one:

- sell a low-ticket guide
- pitch a service
- grow the email list
- push an affiliate product
- book calls
- invite paid membership

Write the bridge before publishing:

"If this helped, the next step is [offer] because [why it solves the next problem]."

Do not wait until the end to figure out what the content is selling. If there is no next step, attention leaks out of the room.
