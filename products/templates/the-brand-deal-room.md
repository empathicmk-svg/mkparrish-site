## Section One: The Partnership Position

Before you make a media kit, write the sentence that explains why a brand should care.

Use this frame:

**I help [specific audience] discover [specific kind of product or idea] through [your content style or authority], so brands get [measurable or emotional result].**

Examples:

- I help first-time apartment decorators find useful, beautiful home products through warm short-form reviews, so brands get trust before the click.
- I help ambitious women rebuild their personal style after major life changes through try-ons, story-driven captions, and honest fit notes, so brands get buyers who feel seen.
- I help early-stage founders understand marketing tools through plain-English demos, so brands get qualified attention instead of empty reach.

Your partnership position should not sound like "I make lifestyle content." It should explain the commercial reason your audience pays attention.

### The Three Proof Types

Put these in your kit before follower count.

1. **Audience proof:** who watches, reads, saves, replies, and buys.
2. **Content proof:** examples of posts that carried trust, teaching, taste, or conversion.
3. **Commercial proof:** affiliate revenue, clicks, DMs, sellouts, waitlist growth, coupon use, or testimonials.

If you do not have sales proof yet, use behavior proof. Saves, replies, link clicks, and repeated questions are all signs that your audience moves.

## Section Two: The Media Kit Page

Build one clean page first. A beautiful 12-page deck will not save unclear positioning.

### The One-Page Structure

Use this order:

1. **Name and partnership position**
2. **Audience snapshot**
3. **Content lanes**
4. **Partnership menu**
5. **Proof**
6. **Rates or "starting at"**
7. **Contact**

### Copy Blocks To Steal

**Header:**
MK Parrish-style frame: "I make [category] content for [audience] who [specific desire/problem]."

**Audience:**
"My audience comes for [topic], stays for [voice/point of view], and trusts me because [reason]."

**Content lanes:**
"I partner best with brands in [3 categories]. My strongest formats are [formats]. I am not a fit for [boundaries]."

**Proof line:**
"Recent sponsored or affiliate content has driven [result], [result], and [result]."

**If you do not have paid proof yet:**
"Organic content repeatedly drives [behavior], which makes sponsored integration feel native instead of bolted on."

Do not pad. A brand manager is scanning for fit, risk, and speed. Give them all three.

## Section Three: The Rate Card

Rates are not morality. They are math plus confidence.

Start with a simple menu:

| Offer | Includes | Starting Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Short-form video | 1 TikTok/Reel, 3-month organic usage | $___ |
| Story set | 3-5 frames, link sticker, 24-hour live window | $___ |
| Carousel | 6-8 slides, caption, organic usage | $___ |
| UGC package | 3 raw videos for brand channels, no organic post | $___ |
| Bundle | 1 video + story set + usage rights | $___ |

### The Rate Formula

Use this as a starting point:

**Base creation fee + audience access + usage rights + exclusivity + complexity = quote.**

Base creation fee pays for production. Audience access pays for posting to your account. Usage rights pay for the brand using your content on their channels or in ads. Exclusivity pays for categories you cannot promote. Complexity pays for extra rounds, travel, scripts, revisions, or legal review.

### Usage Rights Questions

Ask these before quoting:

- Will the brand repost organically?
- Will the brand run this as paid media?
- Which platforms will it run on?
- How long do they want usage rights?
- Do they need raw files?
- Do they need whitelisting or creator licensing?
- Is category exclusivity required?

If they cannot answer, quote the basic organic package first and price paid usage separately.

## Section Four: Pitch Scripts

### Warm Pitch

Subject: Partnership idea for [Brand]

Hi [Name],

I have been using/following [specific product or campaign] and had a content idea I think would land with my audience of [audience].

The angle: [one-sentence idea].

Why it fits: my audience regularly asks about [proof], and this would let me show [product] in a way that feels useful, specific, and native to my content.

I can send a one-page media kit and a few package options if partnerships are on your roadmap this month.

Best,
[Name]

### Follow-Up

Hi [Name],

Floating this back up in case it got buried. I still think [angle] is a strong fit for [brand] because [audience reason].

Happy to send a tighter concept or rate card if useful.

Best,
[Name]

### Inbound Reply

Hi [Name],

Thank you for thinking of me. This sounds aligned.

Before I quote, can you confirm:

- deliverables
- timeline
- usage rights
- whether paid media/whitelisting is included
- exclusivity requirements
- budget range

Once I have those, I can send the cleanest package recommendation.

Best,
[Name]

## Section Five: The Partnership Tracker

Use a simple table:

| Brand | Contact | Fit | Idea | Status | Next Step | Quote | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand A | Name/email | High | Demo + story | Pitched | Follow up Friday | $___ | Asked about usage |

Statuses:

- Watchlist
- Pitched
- Replied
- Brief requested
- Quoted
- Negotiating
- Contract sent
- Invoice sent
- Content live
- Follow-up sent

The tracker is not admin. It is revenue memory. Creators lose money when every partnership lives in a different app, thread, or mood.
